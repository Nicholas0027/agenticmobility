Agents and agency¶

Three independent settings¶

For each kind of agent you set, independently of the other kinds:

  • the decision model: who decides. A rule, a behavioural model, an optimiser, a language model or a remote service.

  • the agency: what it may decide. A level from L0 to L4, or any subset of permissions.

  • what it has to go on: observation, memory and interaction, see Observation, memory, interaction.

Agency is decision authority: the set of actions the environment offers an agent at a decision point. An agent without authority there takes the default action and its decision model is not consulted. The same language model can therefore be a level-0 or a level-4 vehicle, and a rule can be compared with a language model under identical authority.

The seven built-in kinds¶

Kind

L0 (non-agentic)

What agency adds

traveler (human)

habitual mode, follows the vehicle, waits

L1 choose mode · L2 accept or decline the vehicle’s suggestions · L3 give up waiting · L4 re-plan

vehicle (private car, one per car-owning traveller)

route fixed at departure

L1 re-route on traffic · L2 weigh time against money for its owner · L3 propose park-and-ride to the owner · L4 manage its own charging

ondemand (a fleet that carries travellers who call a car)

takes every request, fixed route, waits where it dropped its passenger

L1 re-route on traffic · L2 turn a request down · L4 decide, while free, where to wait

bus (transit vehicle)

leaves when boarding is done

L1 hold at stops · L2 skip a stop nobody needs

evcs (charging station)

fixed tariff, first come first served

L1 dynamic price · L2 choose who charges next

signal (traffic lights of a junction)

fixed-time plan

L1 keep or end a green by the queues at its own junction · L2 see the next junctions and act on what the orchestrator asks for

operator (the orchestrator)

no run-time control

L1 publish traffic advisories · L2 transit priority, a cordon charge, requests to traffic lights

Level names are those of Table I in arXiv:2507.04996. The mapping of permissions to levels is this simulator’s simplified reading of that table.

Writing agency in a scenario¶

agents:
  vehicle:
    agency: L3                                   # a level
  bus:
    agency: [hold]                               # or a list of permissions
  traveler:
    agency: L0
    mix: [{share: 0.3, agency: L4}]              # 30% of travellers at L4, the rest at L0

An event can change agency during a run (type: set_agency).

Kinds interact¶

A level-4 vehicle chooses a station by the price a level-1 station set; an orchestrator’s advisory lets even level-0 vehicles avoid a crash. What one kind’s agency is worth therefore depends on the others: on the testbed, cars that re-route raise traffic speed by 4 mph when nobody else acts, and by almost nothing once the orchestrator publishes an advisory. See Experiments.
